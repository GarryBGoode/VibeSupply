## 4. Inductor and controller current sense

- Inductor: Coilcraft SER2918H-682KL, 6.8 µH, Isat 45.9 A, DCR ≤ 2.86 mΩ (hot ≈ 3.72 mΩ).
- Worst ripple 5.9 A pp → peak at 20 A output 22.9 A.
- R_SENSE = 2.5 mΩ → cycle-by-cycle peak limit 26.4 / 30.0 / 33.6 A (min/typ/max).
  Min limit leaves 3.5 A headroom above the 20 A peak (the MCU-set ITH clamp is the working limit, this is the backstop); max limit (33.6 A) is below the inductor Isat (45.9 A).
- R_SENSE dissipation at 20 A: 1.00 W → 2512 metal-strip, ≥2 W, Kelvin-connected.
- Min on-time 80 ns → min duty 2.4 %: below 1.15 V out (48 V in) / 0.36 V (15 V in) the controller pulse-skips (higher ripple, still regulates). Lower V_in via PD helps; not possible on a 48 V DC input.

- Worst ~10 A RMS at D≈0.5. Proposal: 10× 4.7 µF 100 V X7S/X7R 1210 (≈20 µF effective at 48 V, ~1 A RMS each) right at the half-bridge + bulk 100 V aluminium electrolytic (≥100 µF, ESR 0.1–0.3 Ω) for damping.
- Input ripple (ceramics only, 20 A, D=0.5): ≈ 0.83 V pp.
- Damping vs. ~1 µH of source cable: Z0 = √(L/C) ≈ 0.22 Ω → bulk cap ESR in that range damps the input resonance.
- PD rule: keep ≤10 µF on raw VBUS *before* the sink switch (TPS26750 recommends 1–10 µF); bulk sits after the LM74800.

## 6. Output capacitors and post-filter

- C1 (after main L): 6× 4.7 µF 100 V 1210 (≈11 µF eff. at 46 V).
- C1 damper: 100 µF, ESR/series R ≈ 0.1 Ω.
- Post filter: 0.47 µH (≥25 A, 1.7 mΩ) → C2: 6× 4.7 µF 100 V 1210 + 100 µF 63 V polymer (24 mΩ).
- Ripple (fundamental, worst ΔIL 5.9 A pp): at C1 ≈ 259 mV pp, at the output ≈ **5.9 mV pp** (target ≤ 20 mV).
- Post-filter resonance ≈ 22.0 kHz (with the electrolytic); CV-loop crossover must stay well below → see §9.
- Energy in output caps at 50 V ≈ 0.15 J (dumped into a short before CC takes over — small vs. a DP100-class supply).

## 7. Sensing and setpoint scaling

- CV divider 1/20 (e.g. 190 k / 10 k, 0.1 %, 25 ppm): 50 V → 2.50 V.
  STM32G474 12-bit DAC on VREFBUF 2.9 V → 14.2 mV per LSB at the output.
- CC: INA240A2 (gain 50) across the 2.5 mΩ inductor shunt (see §9 for why) → 0.125 V/A; 20 A → 2.50 V = DAC full scale, 5.7 mA per LSB.
  INA240 offset ±25 µV max → ±10 mA error; firmware trims the setpoint from the INA228 reading.
- Metering: INA228 on the same 1 mΩ shunt; ±40.96 mV range (ADCRANGE=1) → up to 40 A, LSB ≈ 48 µA with CURRENT_LSB = 25 A/2^19; bus voltage 195 µV/LSB.
- Shunt dissipation at 20 A: output 0.40 W, input 0.40 W → 2512 4-terminal (Kelvin) 1 mΩ, ≥1 W.
- Option for later: external dual 16-bit DAC (e.g. DAC80502) → 0.76 mV / 0.4 mA resolution. Leave a footprint.

## 8. Regen clamp / down-programmer

| Vout V | clamp I A (on) | max absorb W (100 % on) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 2.5 | 12 |
| 12 | 6.0 | 72 |
| 24 | 12.0 | 288 |
| 36 | 18.0 | 648 |
| 50 | 25.0 | 1250 |

- R = 2.0 Ω thick-film power resistor (Vishay LTO100, TO-247, 100 W) switched by a 100 V FET, hysteretic on Vout > Vset + margin; firmware integrates energy and derates on the NTC.
- Can absorb 50 W down to √(50·2.0) ≈ 10.0 V; below that less (V²/R), which matches smaller regen at low voltage.
- **Rating (agreed): burst-only.** 50 W for up to ~10 s (≈500 J), then firmware limits to ~10 W average
  and turns the output off with a warning if regen keeps coming. It's a safety net, not a brake resistor.
- The resistor (TO-247 thick film, e.g. Vishay LTO100 class) bolts to the main heatsink: 500 J into ~150 g of aluminium ≈ 4 K rise, so no separate clamp heatsink is needed.
- Firmware: integrate clamp energy (V²/R × duty), track heatsink NTC, enforce the budget; hardware comparator does the switching.

## 9. Loop sanity check (frequency domain)

Model: current-mode modulator as a transconductance Gi = 1/(A_cs·R_SENSE) with the Ridley sampling double pole at fsw/2
(Q = 0.64, i.e. adequate slope compensation), feeding C1 (+ damper) → post-filter L → C2 ∥ electrolytic ∥ load.
External amps: type-II (integrator + zero + HF pole). Starting values only.

Reading:
- Sensing the load current *after* the post filter puts the lightly damped L_f–C1 series resonance inside the CC loop;
  near a short it's unstable. Sensing the inductor current gives a load-independent, well-behaved plant.
  The CC loop then regulates the average inductor current = DC output current; the 1 mΩ output shunt (INA228)
  stays for metering and a slow firmware trim of the CC setpoint.
- The C1 damper (RC across C1) tames the post-filter resonance for the CV loop.
